### What I Learned

#### 1. Ship Fast with AI, Not by AI

* **Accelerating the development process**: AI can effectively support coding, testing, and debugging, helping reduce the time required to complete tasks.
* **The role of humans**: Activities such as code review, integration, deployment, and operations still require engineers' control and decision-making.
* **Standardizing knowledge**: Frequently recurring issues can be converted into automated validation rules such as Lint Rules or CI Checks, reducing the need for manual handling.
* **Living Wiki**: Build shared documentation using Markdown so that both humans and AI Agents can reference, update, and use the knowledge during development.
* **Maintaining responsibility**: AI plays a supporting role, while engineers still need to understand, verify, and take responsibility for the final product.

#### 2. AI Trends – Infrastructure Engineer

* **AIOps**: Applying AI to infrastructure operations to detect anomalies, analyze data, and predict potential incidents.
* **AI-driven IaC**: Using AI to assist in creating and validating Infrastructure as Code such as Terraform or CDK, while also identifying inconsistent configurations.
* **FinOps**: AI can analyze Cloud resource usage and provide recommendations for cost optimization and more efficient resource utilization.
* **Agentic Infrastructure**: Infrastructure is evolving from AI-assisted models toward greater automation and eventually self-managing capabilities.
* **Explainability**: It is important to understand the reasoning behind AI decisions to ensure control, evaluation, and accountability.

#### 3. From AI Trends to Business Value

* **AI, GenAI, and AI Agents**: Learned about three levels of AI applications in Sales activities, ranging from task assistance to the ability to automatically perform certain tasks.
* **Time optimization**: AI can reduce the time spent on repetitive tasks such as searching for customer information, conducting market research, and preparing documents.
* **AI-supported sales journey**: AI can support the entire process, from prospecting and research to understanding customer needs, developing proposals, and post-sales support.
* **Reducing time to value**: Applying AI can accelerate activities such as research and proposal development, thereby improving Sales productivity.
* **The human factor**: Relationships, trust, communication, and negotiation remain essential for building relationships and closing deals.

#### 4. OpenClaw – Open-Source AI Agents

* **Agent Runtime**: Learned about OpenClaw as an environment for operating and managing AI Agents rather than simply viewing it as an independent application.
* **Multi-layer architecture**: Explored architectural layers ranging from AI model connectivity and the Agent execution loop to Coding Agent, Gateway, Memory, Cron, and Session.
* **Agent Loop**: Understood how an AI Agent goes through a cycle of reasoning, using tools, and selecting the next action based on the results received.
* **Risks from Skills/Plugins**: Became aware of security issues that may arise when Agents use unsafe Skills or Plugins, particularly when exploited through Prompt Injection.
* **AI Agent Memory**: Understood how the Memory mechanism can be used to provide additional information to the Prompt, allowing the Agent to maintain and use information during its operation.

### Lessons Learned

- This event showed me that the greatest impact of AI is not simply making individual tasks faster, but rather requiring us to rethink how work is organized around AI. From the "Ship Fast with AI, Not by AI" presentation, I learned that writing code faster is meaningless if code review, testing, and deployment cannot scale accordingly. Real productivity comes from investing in shared infrastructure such as lint rules and living documentation, rather than relying solely on AI-generated output.

- The presentation on infrastructure showed me that applying AI to cloud engineering is not only about automation. It also represents a shift in roles, from manually operating systems to designing and monitoring increasingly self-managing systems. This also introduces new responsibilities, particularly regarding explainability and security, because trusting an AI decision requires understanding why that decision was made.

- From the OpenClaw presentation, I learned that as agents gain more autonomy and access to more tools, their capabilities are developing faster than the safety "guardrails" surrounding them. This highlights the importance of carefully designing memory and permissions for AI agents.

- Finally, the presentation about Sales reinforced an important balance: AI is highly effective at eliminating repetitive work, but trust, relationships, and negotiation still depend heavily on humans. Overall, this event helped me understand that AI should not be viewed as a replacement for human skills or judgment. Instead, it is a tool that creates real value when combined with human supervision and well-designed systems around it.

### Experience at the Event

Participating in the AWS Vietnam Community Meetup was a valuable experience in my learning journey about AI and Cloud. The event brought together many technical presentations covering both infrastructure and AI applications in business.

I particularly enjoyed the "OpenClaw" presentation because it demonstrated how an open-source project can evolve from a simple WhatsApp forwarding bot into a complete agent runtime, surpassing React in GitHub stars within just a few months. The "Ship Fast with AI, Not by AI" presentation also inspired me by showing that writing code faster with AI is not enough. Real speed comes from investing in review, testing, and knowledge sharing across the team.

In addition to the technical presentations, the discussion of AI trends from the perspective of an Infrastructure Engineer helped me better understand how AIOps, FinOps, and AI-driven IaC are reshaping daily cloud operations. I also found the presentation about AI applications in Sales very interesting, as it demonstrated how AI can free professionals from repetitive preparation tasks so they can focus on building genuine customer relationships.

Overall, the event gave me an opportunity to connect with Cloud and AI professionals, exchange ideas with other participants, and gain a clearer understanding of the industry's direction, from AI-assisted coding to fully agentic infrastructure.
